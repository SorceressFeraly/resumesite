Heart Electric

At Modoyo, I was hired to design and own the implementation of the meta-game aspects and live service of the online hero shooter Heart Electric.
My responsibilities included the design of the progression systems, player economy, and live-ops frameworks.
I worked close with other disciplines and implemented features directly in Unreal Engine 5.

Progression Systems

Progression Feature

The progression systems in Heart Electric aimed to facilitate vertical power progression in the short-term (~30h) and horizontal progression mid-term (~300h).

To this end, the progression systems utilized asynchronous progression loops based around the gated ladders and reward structures.

  • Account Level
  • Character Contracts
  • Seasonal Shop
  • Gun Levels
  • Crafting
  • Meta-missions
  • XP Distribution
  • Token Distribution

Loadout Builder

Engagement Feature

The Loadout is a combination of strategic choices that the player can bring into a match.

The goals of the Loadout system was to allow the player to tinker with the gameplay in a sandbox manner, encourage the players to find creative ways of finding new plays, create social discourse & new content types to increase the creativity space.

From a meta and progression perspective, the Loadout was intended as the main engagement feature for the first 30h + 300h of gameplay.

Access to gameplay features such as new guns and gameplay-altering perks would be presented in the first 30h as a vertical power progression, after which a more detailed horizontal system of sidegrades would open up to be explored and unlock for the next 300h of gameplay.

Practice Range

Onboarding Feature

The practice range was an onboarding feature created to decrease initial churn during playtests.

I was responsible for planning and designing the feature along with setting the scope and coordinating the team through Jira. The team was given three weeks to create a level that could be accessed offline and would teach the players the basics of the game.
